# 1 Requirements

## Task and team formation

Each team must choose one of the three challenge projects (Project 1, 2, or 3) described below. Projects should be completed in teams of 3–4 students, and each student should join only one team. We do not require a rigid division of the work into separate parts, but all team members are expected to make substantial and reasonably balanced contributions. At the end of the report PDF, each student should include a brief contribution statement summarizing their main contributions. This statement does not count toward the 6–8 page limit for the main report.

The project should nevertheless be treated as a joint piece of work. Every team member is expected to understand the complete project, including the problem setup, assumptions, main ideas, algorithms, theoretical arguments, experiments, and conclusions. It is not sufficient to understand only the part for which a student was primarily responsible.

## Project timeline and Canvas registration

All deadlines are at 11:59 p.m. (Beijing Time) when a submission is required.

- **December 3, 2026: Presentation and Q&A.** Present your project during the last lecture session of L01.

## Project report and presentation slides

Each team must submit two PDF files: (1) a written project report and (2) a set of presentation slides. Both PDFs are required and must be included in the ZIP file submitted to Canvas by December 2, 2026 at 11:59 p.m. (Beijing Time).

The project report must be 6–8 pages and use the official NeurIPS 2026 template. Do not use the preprint option for the submitted report. References and an optional appendix do not count toward the page limit. The presentation slides must also be submitted in PDF format and should summarize the project’s main ideas and findings.

The main goal is to study one problem deeply. We will evaluate the project based on the same main aspects that are important in a research paper:

- whether the problem is defined precisely and the assumptions and objectives are clear;
- whether the proposed solution, estimator, algorithm, or construction is well motivated;
- whether the mathematical derivations and proofs are correct and as sharp as reasonably possible;
- whether the work provides useful insight into why the result holds, when it is useful, and where its limitations lie;
- whether the writing is clear and the numerical verification, when appropriate, supports the theoretical conclusions.

Numerical experiments are encouraged when they help verify or illustrate the theory, but they should not replace mathematical analysis. A careful and well-understood result for a meaningful special case is preferable to a much more ambitious result that is not fully justified.

## Presentation and Q&A

Each team will give a presentation followed by a Q&A session on December 3, 2026. The exact duration will be determined after the final number of teams is known and announced to the class; the presentation order will be released on the day. Teams may also submit an optional 10-minute recorded presentation in addition to the live presentation.

You may use only the PDF slides submitted to Canvas by the submission deadline. This is the file `presentation_GroupID_taskB-projectID.pdf` described below.

The presentation and Q&A are an important part of the evaluation. The goal is not simply to summarize the report, but to demonstrate a clear understanding of the work. Be prepared to explain the problem setup, assumptions, algorithms, proofs, examples, experiments, and conclusions. The novel parts should be explained clearly and intuitively so that the main ideas and why they work are easy to understand.

Questions may be directed to any team member. Every student should understand all major claims in the report, rather than only the part they contributed to. Students in the same team may receive different final scores based on both individual contributions and the understanding demonstrated in the presentation and Q&A.

## AI tools

We do not restrict the use of AI tools. They may be used for brainstorming, writing, coding, mathematical exploration, literature search, and other parts of the project. The final report must, however, contain a short AI-use statement describing which tools were used and for what purposes.

The team is responsible for every equation, proof, algorithm, claim, reference, experiment, and conclusion in the submitted report. One important piece of advice is not to rely blindly on powerful AI models to produce results that you do not understand. If you use AI tools, make sure you fully understand every detail of the AI-generated content and can independently explain and justify the results.

## Grading standard

The standard maximum project score is 40 points. We evaluate both the overall quality of the project and each student’s individual contribution and understanding. Students in the same team may receive different scores.

Exceptionally strong projects may receive up to 3 bonus points, giving a maximum of 43 points including bonus credit. The standard for bonus credit is intentionally very high: the written report should be of a quality that could reasonably reach the acceptance level of a top machine learning conference such as NeurIPS in terms of technical depth, novelty, clarity, completeness, and overall presentation. A strong presentation and Q&A are also expected for bonus credit.
